## Minimal Formulation

Text-Conditioned Semantic Rendering describes the condition in which a delimited textual structure makes a readable semantic surface possible.

The surface is not produced only by direct instruction.

It is conditioned by the textual body that precedes it.

The text may establish:

```text
vocabulary
→ distinctions
→ recurring terms
→ naming patterns
→ section boundaries
→ exclusions
→ emphasis
→ relation traces
```

A rendered surface may appear as an overview, map, index, reading path, summary, table, catalogue, diagram, or orientation layer.

The central distinction is:

```text
commanded presentation
≠
conditioned formation
```

A semantic surface can form because the text has already stabilized conditions under which certain relations can appear.

The rendered surface remains non-equivalent to the source structure.

---

## Core Characteristics

- **Delimited textual structure** — a bounded body of text that provides the relation field from which a semantic surface can be formed.
- **Textual conditioning** — the role of vocabulary, recurrence, sectioning, emphasis, omission, and naming pattern in shaping what becomes readable.
- **Readable semantic surface** — an organized presentation that allows relations to be inspected, navigated, or discussed.
- **Non-commanded formation** — surface formation that does not require every relation to be explicitly listed or specified in advance.
- **Trace-bound readability** — relations become readable only to the extent that textual traces support their appearance.
- **Non-equivalence** — the rendered surface does not exhaust, replace, or become the source structure.

---

## Structural Logic

Text-Conditioned Semantic Rendering follows a recurring structural sequence:

```text
textual body is delimited
→ vocabulary and distinctions stabilize
→ relation traces accumulate
→ selection becomes possible
→ a readable surface forms
→ the surface appears coherent
→ completeness may be over-attributed
```

The risk appears when the surface is treated as if it were the whole textual structure.

The correct reading is narrower:

```text
textual condition
→ rendered semantic surface
→ bounded non-equivalence
```

The surface depends on the text.

It does not become the text.

---

## Distinction

Text-Conditioned Semantic Rendering is not ordinary summarization.

A summary may condense content.

Text-conditioned rendering describes how textual relations make a readable surface possible.

It is not prompt execution.

A prompt may command an output.

Text-conditioned rendering describes how a source structure conditions what can become readable.

It is not embedding visualization, interface design, semantic mapping, or corpus reconstruction.

The distinction is structural:

```text
direct output generation
= output is produced by instruction or tool operation

text-conditioned semantic rendering
= surface readability is conditioned by textual structure
```

## Application Scope

Text-Conditioned Semantic Rendering may apply wherever delimited textual structures support the formation of readable semantic surfaces.

Relevant contexts include:

- public model lists
- reading paths
- conceptual maps
- knowledge-base summaries
- archive orientation pages
- documentation surfaces
- source indexes
- boundary notes
- relation tables
- AI-readable public corpora

Its use is most appropriate when the question is not only what a surface says, but what textual conditions allow that surface to become readable.

## Non-Applicability

This entry should not be used when:

- the surface is directly specified without meaningful source conditioning
- the source structure is irrelevant to the surface
- the analysis requires a technical visualization method
- the claim depends on complete reconstruction of a corpus
- the surface is being treated as authoritative ontology
- the issue is ordinary summarization alone

The entry does not justify treating a rendered surface as complete.

## Naming Declaration

The term **Text-Conditioned Semantic Rendering** names the structural condition in which a delimited textual structure conditions the formation of a readable semantic surface without requiring every relation to be explicitly commanded, enumerated, or pre-specified.

Within Meta-Writing Ecology, the term refers to the source-side condition of semantic rendering, where textual structure, latent relation, selection pressure, and non-commanded formation shape what becomes readable.

The term does not refer to prompt execution, text-to-image generation, ordinary summarization, embedding visualization, or complete reconstruction of a source corpus.

It marks a shift from:

```text
surface as directly instructed output
```

to:

```text
surface as conditioned formation
```

The term preserves the distinction between a textual structure making rendering possible and a rendered surface becoming equivalent to the source structure.
