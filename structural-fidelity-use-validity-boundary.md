## Minimal Formulation

Structural Fidelity / Use-Validity Boundary describes the condition in which a synthetic, simulated, transformed, compressed, or proxy representation preserves visible features of a source system while failing to preserve the structure required for a specific downstream use.

Core distinction:

    structural fidelity
    ≠
    use-validity

Additional distinctions:

    distributional resemblance
    ≠
    inferential adequacy

    surface preservation
    ≠
    downstream validity

    synthetic similarity
    ≠
    decision support

    proxy realism
    ≠
    use fitness

    visible structure
    ≠
    required structure

    statistical fidelity
    ≠
    causal adequacy

    correlation preservation
    ≠
    mechanism preservation

    access restoration
    ≠
    evidentiary sufficiency

Most compressed:

    looking structurally similar is not the same as being valid for use

Expanded:

A representation may preserve appearance, distribution, category, frequency, correlation, layout, or recognizable output form while losing the relation needed for inference, comparison, prediction, intervention, evaluation, or action.

## Core Characteristics

Structural Fidelity / Use-Validity Boundary treats representation quality and downstream validity as related but non-identical properties.

Its core characteristics include:

  * Source system — the object, dataset, process, record structure, document body, population, mechanism, sequence, or relation field from which a representation is derived.
  * Synthetic or proxy representation — a generated, simulated, transformed, compressed, anonymized, summarized, or substitute object used in place of direct source access.
  * Preserved structure — the visible, measurable, or recognizable features retained by the representation.
  * Use-required structure — the relation that must remain available for a declared downstream task to be valid.
  * Fidelity measure — a comparison that indicates resemblance at a specified layer.
  * Use-validity assessment — an evaluation of whether the representation supports the actual inference, comparison, intervention, decision, or evaluation being attempted.
  * Fidelity–use gap — the distance between the structure tested by the representation metric and the structure required by the use case.
  * Scope expansion — the movement from a narrow fidelity claim to a broader permission for use.
  * Realism pressure — the tendency of a plausible or source-like surface to increase confidence beyond the structure actually verified.

The note does not reject synthetic data, simulations, summaries, models, privacy-preserving transformations, or proxies.

It requires that the preserved structure and the use-required structure be declared separately.

## Structural Logic

Representations are often evaluated by how closely they resemble what they represent.

This comparison may be necessary.

It becomes structurally incomplete when the representation is later used for a purpose that depends on a different relation.

A synthetic dataset may reproduce marginal distributions while losing dependencies.

A summary may preserve main claims while removing qualifications needed for policy interpretation, citation, or professional judgment.

A simulation may generate plausible outcomes without preserving intervention logic.

A proxy may restore access while remaining unsuitable for adjudication, causal analysis, or decision support.

A privacy-preserving transformation may retain aggregate patterns while changing record-level, longitudinal, subgroup, or conditional relations.

The resulting problem is not always ordinary inaccuracy.

The representation may be accurate according to the metric selected.

The error appears when evidence of resemblance is converted into evidence of fitness for a different task.

The central questions are:

    What structure has been preserved?

and:

    What structure does the intended use require?

The relation between those answers determines use-validity.

The central structural sequence is:

    source system is difficult to access, share, inspect, or reuse
    → synthetic or proxy representation is produced
    → visible structure is preserved
    → fidelity metrics appear strong
    → downstream use expands
    → use requires relations not tested by the fidelity metric
    → hidden structural mismatch appears
    → representation becomes valid-looking but use-invalid

The problem does not arise merely because a representation is partial.

It arises when the selection boundary is hidden, forgotten, or treated as irrelevant after the representation enters a downstream task.

## Failure and Aligned Flows

### Failure Flow

    representation generated
    → visible structure preserved
    → similarity appears sufficient
    → downstream use is inferred
    → use-specific relation is not identified
    → fidelity metric substitutes for use validation
    → output supports an invalid inference or decision
    → fidelity is mistaken for validity

In this flow, the representation may continue to perform well according to the metric that produced confidence.

The failure appears at the layer of use.

### Aligned Flow

    representation generated
    → preserved structure is specified
    → intended use is declared
    → use-required relations are identified
    → fidelity is tested against those relations
    → unsupported use is excluded
    → downstream validity remains bounded
    → representation is used only within verified scope

In the aligned flow, fidelity remains meaningful because it stays attached to the layer that was actually tested.

## Primary Patterns

### 1. Fidelity Overextension

A narrow fidelity claim expands into a broader validity claim.

    representation matches source appearance
    → representation is treated as valid for broader use

The representation may have been tested for distributional similarity, categorical coverage, surface realism, or format preservation.

A later use may require ordered dependence, subgroup stability, mechanism continuity, causal sequence, or traceability.

### 2. Surface-Structure Substitution

Visible structure is preserved while the hidden required relation is ignored.

    visible pattern remains
    → required dependency disappears
    → source-like surface is treated as sufficient

The representation may carry recognizable categories, proportions, language, layout, or co-occurrence patterns while failing to carry the relation that gives those features downstream meaning.

### 3. Metric Misplacement

A metric designed for resemblance is treated as if it validated use.

    fidelity metric passes
    → downstream validity is assumed

The metric may be technically sound within its declared scope.

The failure lies in using it to establish a relation it was not designed to test.

### 4. Ordered-Relation Loss

Co-occurrence survives while sequence, dependency, or mechanism is flattened.

    variables appear together
    → order appears irrelevant
    → temporal or conditional structure is lost

This pattern matters when the use depends on event order, causal direction, role sequence, workflow transition, longitudinal relation, or condition-sensitive change.

### 5. Proxy-Use Expansion

A proxy created for access, privacy, exploration, or testing becomes an operational substitute.

    proxy becomes available
    → proxy becomes convenient
    → proxy becomes default
    → proxy enters decision-relevant use

The problem is not proxy existence.

The problem is movement from bounded assistance to unverified replacement.

### 6. Use-Invalid Realism

Realism increases the credibility of an invalid inference.

    representation looks realistic
    → missing relation becomes less visible
    → unsupported use becomes persuasive

A realistic representation can make structural mismatch harder to detect because the surface supplies confidence before use-specific adequacy has been established.

## Distinction

Structural Fidelity / Use-Validity Boundary is not a claim that synthetic representations are invalid.

    synthetic representation
    =
    generated or transformed object preserving selected source properties

    use-invalid representation
    =
    object used for a task requiring structure that has not been preserved or tested

It is not a rejection of approximation.

    approximation
    =
    bounded simplification suitable for a declared purpose

    boundary failure
    =
    approximation used beyond the relation its simplification can support

It is not ordinary inaccuracy.

    inaccuracy
    =
    representation fails at the layer being measured

    fidelity–use mismatch
    =
    representation succeeds at one layer but is treated as valid at another

It is not a universal data-validation framework or causal method.

    structural fidelity / use-validity boundary
    =
    conceptual separation between preserved structure and use-required structure

The narrower condition is:

    a representation appears faithful to a source
    while the downstream use requires structural relations
    that have not been preserved, tested, or declared

## Operational Evaluation

The note supports diagnostic questioning, but it does not provide a complete technical protocol.

Relevant questions include:

  * What structure does the representation preserve?
  * What structure does the intended use require?
  * Are those structures the same?
  * Does the task depend on ordering, dependency, mechanism, causality, subgroup variation, or conditional relation?
  * Has the representation been tested on the actual downstream use?
  * Is a fidelity metric substituting for use-specific validation?
  * What would fail if the representation were similar but use-invalid?

These questions identify the boundary but do not supply universal metrics, thresholds, procedures, or pass–fail criteria.

## Relevant Interpretation Contexts

Structural Fidelity / Use-Validity Boundary may apply wherever a representation is used beyond the layer at which resemblance was established.

Relevant contexts include synthetic and proxy data, simulation and scenario systems, summaries, compressed representations, privacy-preserving transformations, anonymized datasets, benchmarking, generated records, machine-readable abstractions, archive proxies, public data products, and research reuse under limited source access.

General distributions may be preserved while subgroup, longitudinal, causal, or record-level relations remain unresolved. Plausible simulations may support exploration while remaining insufficient for intervention comparison. Summaries may retain topic or claim structure while removing qualifications, source conditions, sequence, or evidentiary boundaries.

Context relevance does not imply a deployed validation tool, audit product, technical standard, compliance system, or domain-specific recommendation.

## Non-Applicability

This note should not be applied to every representation, model, dataset, proxy, simulation, summary, transformation, or approximation.

It does not apply well when:

  * the representation is used only for low-consequence exploration
  * the use case requires only the layer of resemblance already tested
  * the required structure has been explicitly identified and evaluated
  * the representation is not used for inference, decision, comparison, prediction, intervention, or evidentiary support
  * the fidelity measure and use-validity assessment remain separate
  * the representation’s limits are clearly declared and respected
  * unsupported relations are not inferred
  * empirical validation has established adequacy for the actual use

The note should not be used to claim:

    synthetic representation is invalid

or:

    proxy data cannot be useful

or:

    every downstream use requires complete source reconstruction

Its narrower domain is:

    situations in which a representation is faithful at one layer
    but is used for a task requiring a different or deeper layer of preservation

## Limitations

  * Use-specific rather than universal — assessment requires a declared task, relation, consequence, and evidence condition.
  * Metric dependence — the note does not provide a universal fidelity metric, utility score, adequacy threshold, or benchmark.
  * Causal claims require separate methods — the framework may identify missing causal structure but does not establish causality.
  * No rejection of approximation — approximate representations may be adequate for bounded exploratory or low-consequence purposes.
  * Validation remains empirical — conceptual separation cannot replace testing the actual downstream use.
  * Completeness is not required — use-validity depends on sufficient preservation for the task, not exhaustive source reconstruction.

## Naming Declaration

Structural Fidelity / Use-Validity Boundary originates within Meta-Writing Ecology as a structural term for the boundary between a representation’s apparent similarity to a source and its validated adequacy for a specific downstream use.

The term structural fidelity refers to the preservation of visible, measurable, or recognizable features of a source system.

These may include distributions, categories, proportions, frequencies, correlations, sequences, labels, syntax, layout, output forms, temporal ranges, or other observable relations.

Structural fidelity is always fidelity to something.

A fidelity claim must therefore identify the layer, feature, relation, or comparison under which resemblance is asserted.

The term use-validity refers to whether the representation can legitimately support the task for which it is used.

That task may involve inference, comparison, prediction, intervention analysis, evaluation, decision-making, policy formation, publication, citation, research reuse, or operational action.

Use-validity does not require preservation of every property of a source.

It requires preservation or independent support of the structure needed by the declared use.

The term fidelity–use gap refers to a mismatch between the structure demonstrated by a fidelity assessment and the structure required by the downstream task.

The term proxy-use expansion refers to movement from a bounded purpose such as access, privacy, testing, or exploration into a broader evidentiary or operational role.

The term does not refer to a specific discipline, technical method, dataset type, model provider, statistical technique, simulation platform, or empirical field. It does not define a universal validation method or claim that synthetic representations are inherently invalid.

Within Meta-Writing Ecology, the term functions as a Cross-supporting boundary note.

It clarifies a recurring failure in which resemblance, accessibility, statistical similarity, synthetic realism, or source-like appearance is mistaken for evidentiary adequacy or downstream fitness.

It should be used when a representation appears faithful to a source system but the intended use requires structural relations that have not been preserved, tested, or declared.

This document constitutes the first OSF external-facing version of Structural Fidelity / Use-Validity Boundary as a boundary note for examining representation similarity, preserved structure, use-required relations, downstream validity, metric placement, proxy-use expansion, and the non-equivalence between apparent fidelity and validated fitness for use.
