## Minimal Formulation

Generation-Condition Disclosure–Reproducibility Cross describes the condition in which a generated output is treated as reproducibility-sensitive evidence while the conditions that produced it remain absent, hidden, unstable, under-described, or non-transferable.

The Cross begins from the distinction:

```text
generated output
≠
reproducible evidence
```

A generated text, classification, annotation, summary, simulation, or analytic result may appear stable.

But the output may depend on conditions that are not visible in the output itself:

```text
system identity
access mode
interface context
instruction context
configuration context
memory or customization state
input boundary
post-processing
validation procedure
platform dependency
```

The Cross appears when output circulation and generation-condition opacity create false reproducibility expectations.

---

## Core Characteristics

- **Generated output** — a model-mediated result that appears as a discrete research, analytic, or public-facing object.
- **Generation condition** — the production field that shaped the output, including system, interface, instruction, access, configuration, and validation conditions.
- **Reproducibility pressure** — the expectation that an output can be inspected, repeated, compared, or evaluated as research evidence.
- **Disclosure boundary** — the limit between reportable production conditions and full operational exposure.
- **Output variability** — the instability or non-transferability of generated results across systems, sessions, configurations, or contexts.
- **Operational non-disclosure** — the principle that condition awareness does not require exposing full prompts, private materials, internal workflow, or registry infrastructure.

---

## Structural Logic

The Cross follows a recurring structural sequence:

```text
model-mediated output is produced
→ output appears discrete and stable
→ output enters research or public workflow
→ reproducibility pressure attaches
→ generation conditions remain opaque
→ output is mistaken for reproducible evidence
→ disclosure demand expands
→ operational boundary must be preserved
```

The central asymmetry is:

```text
output visibility
≠
condition reproducibility
```

A visible output may still lack the production conditions required for reproducibility-sensitive interpretation.

---

## Distinction

Generation-Condition Disclosure–Reproducibility Cross is not a claim that generated outputs are invalid.

It is not a claim that all generation conditions must be fully disclosed.

It is not a prompt-engineering method, model-evaluation benchmark, compliance framework, or reproducibility standard.

The distinction is structural:

```text
full operational disclosure
≠
condition-aware reproducibility boundary
```

A workflow may need to disclose enough generation conditions to prevent false reproducibility claims.

That does not mean it must expose complete prompts, private source material, internal registry structures, or full operational infrastructure.

---

## Application Scope

This Cross may apply wherever generated outputs are treated as inspectable, reusable, citable, comparable, or reproducibility-sensitive.

Relevant contexts include:

- model-mediated research notes
- generated summaries
- automated classifications
- synthetic annotations
- AI-assisted coding or analysis
- public-facing generated documents
- institutional knowledge workflows
- model-mediated data transformation
- generated evidence surfaces
- reproducibility-sensitive reports

Its use is most appropriate when the output is being evaluated beyond ordinary usefulness and is entering a reproducibility or evidence relation.

---

## Non-Applicability

This Cross should not be used when:

- the generated output is not treated as evidence
- no reproducibility claim is being made
- the output is openly provisional or exploratory
- the issue is ordinary factual error
- the relevant production conditions are already adequately reported
- the analysis demands full operational disclosure rather than bounded condition awareness

The Cross does not require public exposure of full operational materials.

---

## Public Orientation and Method Boundary

Generation-condition awareness is compatible with operational non-disclosure.

This public entry does not provide:

- prompt histories
- private source material
- backend workflow
- internal registry details
- full toolchain records
- reproducibility checklists
- audit procedures

It defines the boundary at which reproducibility claims become structurally sensitive to generation conditions.

## Naming Declaration

The term **Generation-Condition Disclosure–Reproducibility Cross** names the structural condition in which generated output cannot be treated as reproducibility-sensitive evidence when the conditions that produced it are absent, hidden, unstable, or under-described.

Within Meta-Writing Ecology, the term refers to the intersection of generation condition, output variability, reproducibility pressure, disclosure boundary, and operational non-disclosure.

The term does not refer to a specific model, provider, platform, reporting checklist, technical implementation method, audit standard, or disciplinary reproducibility rule.

It marks a shift from:

```text
output-based reproducibility judgment
```

to:

```text
condition-aware reproducibility boundary
```

The term preserves the distinction between disclosing enough generation conditions to prevent false reproducibility claims and exposing full operational details, prompts, private source material, internal workflow, or registry-level infrastructure.
