## Minimal Formulation

Surface-Bounded Semantic Rendering describes the condition in which latent semantic relations become readable through a bounded surface.

The surface may appear as:

```text
map
index
table
catalogue
diagram
orientation layer
reading path
diagnostic frame
```

The rendered surface makes relations inspectable.

It does not become equivalent to the full semantic field, source structure, or relational system from which it is drawn.

The central distinction is:

```text
readable surface
≠
complete field
```

A surface can show pattern without showing origin.

It can show proximity without establishing dependency.

It can make a field readable without preserving the full structure that gave rise to it.

---

## Core Characteristics

- **Bounded surface** — a readable presentation formed through selection, delimitation, ordering, compression, and viewpoint.
- **Semantic field** — the broader relational space from which the surface draws but does not exhaust.
- **Rendering** — the formation of a readable surface from relations that are not directly visible as a whole.
- **Boundary control** — the preservation of limits around what the surface can and cannot claim.
- **Non-equivalence** — the surface is not the field, source, ontology, archive, or full system.
- **Readability risk** — readability can be mistaken for completeness, authority, or structural recognition.

---

## Structural Logic

Surface-Bounded Semantic Rendering follows a recurring structural sequence:

```text
latent relations exceed direct inspection
→ a surface is formed
→ selection and compression make relations readable
→ readability creates apparent coherence
→ the surface is treated as representative
→ non-equivalence risk appears
```

The surface is useful because it is bounded.

The same boundary that makes readability possible also limits what the surface can claim.

The correct relation is:

```text
semantic field
→ bounded rendering
→ readable surface
≠
complete system
```

---

## Distinction

Surface-Bounded Semantic Rendering is not visualization alone.

A visualization may display data.

Surface-bounded rendering describes the boundary condition under which a readable semantic surface becomes interpretable but non-equivalent.

It is not mapping alone.

A map may imply coverage.

Surface-bounded rendering emphasizes that coverage remains partial.

It is not indexing, summarization, interface design, or ontology construction.

The distinction is structural:

```text
representation as completion
≠
surface as bounded rendering
```

---

## Application Scope

Surface-Bounded Semantic Rendering may apply wherever complex semantic relations are made readable through a public or navigational surface.

Relevant contexts include:

- relation maps
- model atlases
- public knowledge surfaces
- reading paths
- conceptual diagrams
- archive indexes
- AI-readable documentation
- semantic maps
- classification tables
- interface summaries

Its use is most appropriate when a surface is useful but may be mistaken for a complete system.

## Non-Applicability

This entry should not be used when:

- a surface is openly partial and not mistaken for completeness
- the issue is only visual style
- no semantic relation is being rendered
- the surface has no interpretive or navigational role
- the analysis requires a technical visualization method
- the claim depends on treating the surface as ontology

The entry does not make readability suspicious by default.

## Naming Declaration

The term **Surface-Bounded Semantic Rendering** names the structural condition in which a readable semantic surface is formed under boundary constraints without becoming equivalent to the semantic field, source structure, or relational system from which it is drawn.

Within Meta-Writing Ecology, the term refers to the surface-side condition of semantic rendering, where selection, projection, boundary control, and non-equivalence preservation shape what becomes readable.

The term does not refer to a visualization method, graphic style, interface design technique, complete ontology, complete semantic map, or full reconstruction of an underlying system.

It marks a shift from:

```text
readable surface as complete representation
```

to:

```text
readable surface as bounded rendering
```

The term preserves the distinction between making relations visible and claiming that the rendered surface exhausts the field from which those relations are drawn.
