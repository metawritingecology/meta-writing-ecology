## Minimal Formulation

Provenance–Validity Separation describes the structural condition in which an object's origin, attribution, custody, generation history, transformation record, source trace, credential status, or provenance pathway can be established while validity remains unresolved.

The model begins from the distinction:

```text
provenance
≠
validity
```

and:

```text
traceable origin
≠
valid claim
```

and:

```text
authenticity
≠
semantic adequacy
```

In compressed form:

```text
object appears
→ provenance is attached
→ origin becomes traceable
→ trust increases
→ validity is inferred
→ content adequacy remains untested
→ provenance-validity gap appears
```

Most compressed:

```text
Provenance can stabilize origin without stabilizing validity.
```

Diagnostic question:

```text
What does the provenance prove,
and what does it leave unproven?
```

## Core Characteristics

The Provenance–Validity Separation Model treats provenance as necessary but structurally incomplete.

Its core characteristics include:

- **Origin stabilization** — provenance can identify where an object came from, who produced it, when it was generated, which system recorded it, or what transformation pathway it followed.
- **Validity gap** — truth, adequacy, semantic fidelity, evidentiary sufficiency, contextual fit, and operational fitness remain unresolved after origin is stabilized.
- **Traceability overextension** — source traceability is treated as stronger assurance than it structurally supports.
- **Authenticity overtrust** — authenticity at the level of origin or production is mistaken for adequacy, truth, or evidentiary relevance.
- **Semantic adequacy problem** — a representation, summary, paraphrase, citation, or generated output may be correctly attributed while failing to preserve the meaning-bearing structure required for use.
- **Credential over-reading** — metadata, signatures, source marks, content credentials, or chain-of-custody records are interpreted as broader validity signals.
- **Responsibility drift** — provenance may identify origin while leaving responsibility for interpretation, claim support, validity checking, or downstream use unresolved.
- **Residual confidence** — an object may retain credibility after correction because its source remains traceable.

The model does not reject provenance systems.

It prevents provenance from being treated as a substitute for validity.

## Structural Logic

Provenance–Validity Separation follows a recurring sequence:

```text
object formation
→ provenance attachment
→ origin stabilization
→ trust transfer
→ validity deferral
→ operational use
→ validity gap appears
→ reactive correction
→ residual confidence
```

A system may know:

```text
where the object came from
who produced it
when it was generated
what device, model, platform, institution, or process produced it
what metadata accompanies it
what transformations it underwent
what chain of custody can be reconstructed
```

while still not knowing:

```text
whether the object is true
whether the representation is adequate
whether the interpretation is justified
whether the citation supports the claim
whether the summary preserves the source
whether the media object is contextually valid
whether the dashboard value is a sufficient proxy
whether the agentic trace justifies the action
whether the object is fit for the use being made of it
```

The model names the gap between those two layers.

The failure does not require false provenance.

It can occur even when provenance is correct.

## Central Distinctions

### Provenance Is Not Validity

```text
provenance
≠
validity
```

Provenance identifies origin, source, custody, attribution, generation path, or transformation sequence.

Validity concerns whether the object, claim, representation, output, media artifact, classification, trace, or document holds under the conditions of use.

---

### Traceability Is Not Truth

```text
traceability
≠
truth
```

Traceability makes an object more inspectable.

It does not make the object true.

A traceable claim remains subject to factual, contextual, semantic, procedural, and evidentiary evaluation.

---

### Authenticity Is Not Adequacy

```text
authenticity
≠
adequacy
```

An authentic source may still be insufficient evidence.

An authentic media object may still be misleading in context.

An authentic document may still be irrelevant to the use being made of it.

---

### Chain of Custody Is Not Claim Support

```text
chain of custody
≠
claim support
```

A preserved record does not automatically establish that the record supports a specific claim.

Custody stabilizes transfer.

Claim support requires evidentiary relation.

---

### Attribution Is Not Interpretation

```text
attribution
≠
interpretation
```

A source may be correctly identified while the interpretation attached to it is invalid.

---

### Metadata Is Not Meaning

```text
metadata
≠
meaning
```

Metadata can describe origin, timing, format, device, edit state, credential properties, or transformation history.

It does not fully encode meaning, implication, relevance, context, or semantic fidelity.

---

### Generation Trace Is Not Output Validity

```text
generation trace
≠
output validity
```

A model output may include prompt history, retrieval source, model version, or tool path.

That history can describe production without proving that the output should be accepted.

---

### Origin Stability Is Not Referent Stability

```text
origin stability
≠
referent stability
```

A claim may be correctly sourced while its referent relation is weak, incomplete, outdated, distorted, or contextually shifted.

## Primary Failure Patterns

### 1. Traceability-to-Truth Conversion

A traceable origin is treated as evidence that the claim is true.

```text
source is traceable
→ claim appears trustworthy
→ validity checking decreases
```

The system converts origin confidence into truth confidence.

---

### 2. Authentic Media, Invalid Context

A media object is genuine, but its use context is invalid.

```text
image is authentic
→ image is treated as evidence
→ time, place, framing, or interpretation is wrong
```

Authenticity stabilizes production, not context.

---

### 3. Real Source, Unsupported Claim

A citation or source record is real, but the claim attached to it exceeds what the source supports.

```text
source exists
→ citation looks valid
→ cited claim not actually supported
```

The provenance of the citation substitutes for claim verification.

---

### 4. Correct Attribution, Semantic Distortion

A summary, quote, paraphrase, or generated synthesis is correctly attributed to a source, but the meaning has shifted.

```text
source identified
→ summary accepted
→ semantic pressure flattened or reweighted
```

Attribution is stable.

Semantic adequacy fails.

---

### 5. Generated Trace, Invalid Output

An AI output includes a generation trace, prompt history, retrieval source, or model record, but the output remains invalid.

```text
generation path known
→ output appears accountable
→ content remains inaccurate, incomplete, or misleading
```

Production history does not establish output adequacy.

---

### 6. Agentic Log, Unjustified Action

An agentic system produces a trace showing what actions occurred.

The trace may be accurate.

The action may still lack proper authorization, proportionality, validity, safety, or responsibility alignment.

```text
tool path recorded
→ action appears explainable
→ authorization or validity remains unresolved
```

---

### 7. Credential Overtrust

A credential, signature, metadata packet, content credential, or source mark is treated as broader assurance than it provides.

```text
credential confirms origin
→ user infers truth
→ validity gap hidden
```

The credential is over-read.

---

### 8. Provenance-Laundered False Legibility

A provenance signal makes a readable object appear structurally recognized.

```text
object readable
+
source trace present
→ inquiry decreases
→ structural recognition remains incomplete
```

Provenance becomes a surface of false legibility.

---

### 9. Source-Form Attribution Misalignment

The form of an object suggests one kind of source authority while the actual source relation supports a different kind.

```text
institutional form
→ appears officially grounded
→ source relation weaker than form implies
```

The mismatch is not only false source.

It is form-level over-attribution.

---

### 10. Provenance-Accelerated Circulation

An object circulates faster because provenance makes it appear safer to move.

```text
traceable source
→ reduced circulation friction
→ object moves before validity check
```

Provenance can lower movement resistance without settling validity.

---

### 11. Chain-of-Custody Substitution

A preserved chain of custody substitutes for evidentiary sufficiency.

```text
record preserved
→ evidentiary use accepted
→ relevance or claim support underchecked
```

Custody is stable.

Evidence relation remains incomplete.

---

### 12. Validity Responsibility Drift

Because provenance is traceable, responsibility for validity shifts to the visible verifier, reviewer, user, signatory, or downstream actor.

```text
origin is traceable
→ object appears externally supported
→ final user/reviewer becomes responsible for validity
```

Provenance does not itself assign interpretive responsibility.

## AI-Mediated Intensification

AI-mediated systems intensify Provenance–Validity Separation because they produce more objects that are source-marked, generated, logged, retrieved, summarized, attributed, or credentialed without ensuring validity.

### Generated Fluency

AI-generated text may be fluent enough to appear valid, especially when accompanied by citations, references, source links, or retrieval context.

```text
source-linked answer
≠
source-supported answer
```

---

### Synthetic Media Provenance

Synthetic media may contain provenance information indicating generation or transformation.

This helps identify production status.

It does not establish whether the media is being interpreted, framed, circulated, or used appropriately.

---

### Retrieval-Augmented Output

RAG systems can attach retrieved materials to generated outputs.

This improves source visibility.

It does not automatically ensure that the output accurately integrates, ranks, contextualizes, or preserves the retrieved material.

---

### Search Summaries

Search summaries may cite or link to sources.

The summary may still omit, flatten, reweight, or over-stabilize source relations.

```text
traceable source access
≠
semantic adequacy
```

---

### Agentic Traces

Agentic systems may log tool calls, steps, confirmations, and outcomes.

A trace can support process reconstruction.

It does not guarantee that the action was authorized, justified, safe, valid, or responsibility-aligned.

---

### Dashboard Provenance

A metric may be traceable to a system, dataset, sensor, or model.

The metric may still function as an inadequate proxy for the underlying condition.

---

### Automated Classification

A classification may be traceable to a model, rule set, or data source.

The classification may still fail boundary conditions, context, category fit, or downstream fairness.

---

### Institutional Document Generation

An official or institutionally formatted document may have clear authorship and record history.

It may still fail validity if the form, source, process, authority, or claim relation does not cohere.

## Relationship to Adjacent Models

Provenance–Validity Separation is a model within Meta-Writing Ecology. It is adjacent to several existing models but names a distinct structural gap.

- [Reality Consistency Model](./reality-consistency.md) — provides the broader verification frame. Reality Consistency asks whether an object, claim, media artifact, explanation, or generated output continues to hold across source, form, scale, exchange, process, and subject-position as scrutiny expands. Provenance–Validity Separation isolates the narrower gap between origin traceability and content validity.
- [False Legibility Model](./false-legibility.md) — describes readable surfaces accepted before structural recognition. Provenance markers can become false-legibility signals when they make an object appear safer, more official, more trustworthy, or more structurally understood than it is.
- [Premature Circulation Model](./premature-circulation-model.md) — describes movement before recognition, verification, accountability, or structural positioning has stabilized. Provenance–Validity Separation can enable premature circulation when traceability lowers movement friction.
- [Premature Circulation Diagnostics](./premature-circulation-diagnostics.md) — extends the premature circulation problem into diagnostics-facing analysis of AI-mediated information flows. Provenance-bearing objects may move because origin traceability appears to reduce risk before validity has been established.
- [Observer–Representation Boundary](./observer-representation-boundary.md) — describes limits between what is represented and what observers can recognize or govern. Provenance systems produce representations of origin, custody, and transformation; observers may see the provenance representation while lacking access to validity in context.
- Source-Form Attribution Misalignment — concerns cases where a form implies a source relation, authority, or evidentiary status that the actual source relation does not support. Provenance–Validity Separation intersects when provenance identifies one source relation but the form induces a stronger validity inference than that relation can bear.
- Proxy Substitution — describes the condition in which a proxy becomes the object of governance rather than the underlying referent. Provenance can become a proxy for validity when systems optimize around provenance presence rather than claim adequacy.
- Responsibility Alignment Model — distinguishes capacity from ownership, visibility from responsibility, and assistance from transfer. Provenance–Validity Separation can produce responsibility drift when validity responsibility falls on the visible user, verifier, reviewer, or signatory while provenance infrastructure is treated as sufficient support.
- [Cost Visibility and Redistribution Model](./cost-visibility-redistribution.md) — distinguishes total cost from perceived cost. Provenance may reduce visible verification cost by making objects appear easier to trust while the remaining validity work is displaced downstream.
- [Constraint Residue Accumulation Model](./constraint-residue-accumulation-model.md) — describes how prior failures become persistent controls. Provenance-validity confusion can generate warning labels, review gates, credential disclaimers, source-checking requirements, or documentation rules.
- [Constraint Residue Governance](./constraint-residue-governance.md) — describes governance through accumulated failure traces. Provenance–Validity Separation intersects when provenance-related controls accumulate without repairing the separation between origin and validity.
- [Boundary Integration Failure](./boundary-integration-failure.md) — describes over-fusion of roles, instructions, authority scopes, and operational layers. Provenance–Validity Separation may collapse when origin verification, truth judgment, evidentiary adequacy, semantic interpretation, and accountability merge into one undifferentiated trust signal.
- [Boundary-Role Segmentation Model](./boundary-role-segmentation-model.md) — describes local coherence with system-level contradiction. Provenance roles and validity roles may remain locally coherent while no role owns the provenance-validity gap.
- [Boundary Failure Diagnostics](./boundary-failure-diagnostics.md) — provides workflow-facing analysis of over-fusion, over-separation, and functional boundary calibration. Provenance–Validity Separation appears when workflows cannot preserve the distinction between origin verification and validity evaluation.
- [Semantic Field Diagnostics](./semantic-field-diagnostics.md) — provides a vocabulary for recognizing drift, projection, boundary substitution, and collapse within meaning-bearing systems. Provenance can become a boundary substitute when source trace replaces validity recognition.
- [Model-Induced Coherence Pressure](./model-induced-coherence-pressure.md) — describes the overproduction of smooth coherence in generative environments. Generated coherence can make provenance-bearing outputs appear more valid than their source relation supports.

## Distinction

Provenance–Validity Separation is not provenance verification.

```text
provenance verification
=
checking origin, custody, edit history, generation path, or attribution

provenance-validity separation
=
examining whether such verification is being mistaken for content validity
```

Provenance–Validity Separation is not fact-checking.

```text
fact-checking
=
evaluation of whether a claim is true or false relative to external evidence

provenance-validity separation
=
structural analysis of whether provenance is being overused as a substitute for that evaluation
```

Provenance–Validity Separation is not authenticity.

```text
authenticity
=
whether an object is genuine at the level of origin, identity, or production

provenance-validity separation
=
whether authenticity is being mistaken for adequacy, truth, or evidentiary relevance
```

Provenance–Validity Separation is not Reality Consistency.

```text
Reality Consistency
=
broader model of cross-dimensional structural endurance

Provenance–Validity Separation
=
narrower model isolating the gap between origin traceability and validity
```

Provenance–Validity Separation is not misinformation.

```text
misinformation
=
false or misleading information

provenance-validity separation
=
gap between origin traceability and content validity
```

The problem may arise even when the source is real, the provenance is correct, and there is no malicious intent.

Provenance–Validity Separation is not media forensics.

```text
media forensics
=
determining whether media was generated, edited, altered, captured, or authenticated

provenance-validity separation
=
asking whether that determination is being overextended into truth, interpretation, or evidentiary adequacy
```

## Non-Applicability

The Provenance–Validity Separation Model should not be applied to every provenance, citation, metadata, or source verification case.

It does not apply well when:

- provenance is the only question being asked
- validity is explicitly outside the use context
- the object is not making or supporting a claim
- the provenance record is used only for archival identification
- the system clearly distinguishes origin from validity
- validity has been independently verified
- the object is openly fictional, symbolic, provisional, or illustrative
- downstream use does not rely on truth, evidence, or semantic adequacy
- provenance and validity are both scoped and bounded
- the issue requires legal evidence standards rather than structural mapping
- the issue is simple source absence rather than provenance-validity confusion

The model should not be used to dismiss provenance systems.

It should not be used to claim that provenance has no value.

It should not be used to imply that validity can be established without source information.

Its domain is narrower:

```text
systems in which traceable origin, authenticity, custody, or attribution
is treated as stronger evidence of validity than it structurally supports
```

## Naming Declaration

The term **Provenance–Validity Separation Model** originates within Meta-Writing Ecology as a structural model for describing the gap between origin traceability and validity in AI-mediated, institutional, platform, evidentiary, and meaning-bearing systems.

The term **provenance** refers to origin, source, authorship, custody, generation history, edit history, transformation pathway, credential status, or attribution record. It does not refer to truth, adequacy, or evidentiary sufficiency by itself.

The term **validity** refers to whether a claim, object, output, representation, summary, media artifact, classification, trace, or document holds under the conditions of use, context, referent, evidence, semantic fidelity, process, and responsibility. It does not mean only formal logical validity.

The term **provenance-validity gap** refers to the condition in which provenance has been stabilized while validity remains unresolved.

The term **semantic adequacy** refers to whether a representation, summary, paraphrase, citation, or generated output preserves the meaning-bearing structure required for its use.

The term **traceability** refers to the ability to follow origin, custody, transformation, or production records. Traceability supports verification but does not complete it.

The term **authenticity** refers to the status of being genuine at the level of origin or production. Authenticity does not automatically establish relevance, truth, or adequacy.

This framework is adjacent to provenance verification, media authentication, content credentials, chain-of-custody systems, fact-checking, digital evidence management, AI governance, platform labeling, content moderation, and archival practice. It is not derived from those fields, although intersections may exist.

This GitHub-facing version does not constitute a provenance verification protocol, media forensics method, legal evidentiary standard, fact-checking method, authenticity certification system, content credential implementation guide, compliance assessment, AI governance checklist, or proprietary client-facing audit workflow.

Within Meta-Writing Ecology, the Provenance–Validity Separation Model intersects with Reality Consistency, False Legibility, Premature Circulation, Premature Circulation Diagnostics, Observer–Representation Boundary, Source-Form Attribution Misalignment, Proxy Substitution, Responsibility Alignment, Cost Visibility and Redistribution, Constraint Residue Accumulation, Constraint Residue Governance, Boundary Integration Failure, Boundary-Role Segmentation, Boundary Failure Diagnostics, Semantic Field Diagnostics, and Model-Induced Coherence Pressure.

It names the condition under which systems must distinguish where something came from from whether it is true, adequate, meaningful, evidentially sufficient, and fit for use.
