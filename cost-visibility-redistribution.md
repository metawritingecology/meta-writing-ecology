## Minimal Formulation

Cost Visibility and Redistribution describes the condition in which total cost becomes decision-relevant only when cost is made visible, represented, decomposable, comparable, or redistributed into an actionable layer.

It begins from the distinction:

```text
perceived cost
≠
total cost
```

A cost may exist materially, temporally, cognitively, institutionally, environmentally, or infrastructurally while remaining outside the system’s decision field.

Systems do not optimize total cost automatically.

They optimize around visible cost.

In compressed form:

```text
total cost is generated
→ cost is partitioned across dimensions and nodes
→ some cost becomes visible
→ some cost remains opaque
→ perceived cost field forms
→ optimization activates or collapses
→ default behavior, redistribution, or lock-in emerges
```

Core insight:

```text
cost that is not visible cannot be optimized

cost that is not represented cannot become part of the decision field
```

---

## Core Characteristics

Cost Visibility and Redistribution treats cost as a structural variable whose effects depend on visibility, representation, comparability, and location within a system.

Its core characteristics include:

- **Total cost** — the full burden generated by a configuration, action, workflow, infrastructure, output, or decision, including spatial, temporal, monetary, cognitive, coordination, verification, infrastructural, environmental, responsibility, and downstream repair costs.
- **Perceived cost** — the portion of total cost that becomes visible, represented, decomposable, comparable, cognitively available, and actionable within a system.
- **Cost opacity** — the condition in which real costs remain outside decision space because they are hidden, deferred, absorbed, isolated, or unrepresented.
- **Cost partitioning** — the division of burden across actors, layers, locations, infrastructures, roles, or future states.
- **Structural externalization** — the movement of cost away from the actor, interface, institution, or layer that benefits from the configuration.
- **Burden transfer** — the relocation of cost to downstream reviewers, users, local communities, infrastructure layers, institutional roles, future maintainers, or other underrepresented nodes.
- **Verification burden** — downstream cost created when low-friction production requires later source checking, validation, alignment, repair, or acceptance review.
- **Layer isolation** — a condition in which a system layer carries cost without being integrated into routine decision-making.
- **Default state lock-in** — stabilization around the lowest perceived-friction configuration because hidden default costs remain excluded while transition costs remain visible.

Cost visibility is not simple transparency.

A cost becomes visible when it becomes structurally available as a decision-relevant, comparable, and actionable variable.

## Structural Logic

Cost Visibility and Redistribution follows a recurring structural sequence:

```text
activity or configuration generates cost
→ total cost is partitioned across dimensions, actors, layers, and time
→ visible cost becomes represented and comparable
→ hidden cost remains opaque, absorbed, deferred, or externalized
→ the perceived cost field shapes available choices
→ optimization occurs around visible cost
→ invisible cost drives burden transfer, repair demand, or default lock-in
```

Optimization requires:

```text
visibility
+ comparability
+ actionable alternatives
```

When these conditions are absent, systems do not optimize.

They default.

Default selection may occur even when total cost is high, because the system repeatedly selects the option with the lowest visible friction.

Over time:

```text
low visible cost
→ repeated selection
→ infrastructure reinforcement
→ alternative pathways thin
→ hidden burden normalizes
→ default state lock-in
```

The system does not remain default because it is optimal.

It remains default because the costs of alternatives are more visible than the costs of continuation.

## Distinction

Cost Visibility is not cost existence.

```text
cost existence
= a burden is real and consequential

cost visibility
= a burden is available to decision space as a represented, comparable, and actionable variable
```

Cost Redistribution is not fair redistribution.

```text
fair redistribution
= a normative allocation of burden

cost redistribution
= the movement, partitioning, displacement, or externalization of burden across agents, layers, systems, locations, or time
```

Cost Visibility is not accounting.

```text
accounting
= selected costs are recorded through defined categories

cost visibility
= costs can enter the perceived cost field and alter decision structure
```

Cost Visibility is not transparency.

```text
transparency
= information is available

cost visibility
= cost is structurally integrated into comparison, action, and optimization
```

Cost Redistribution is not cost removal.

```text
cost removal
= the burden no longer exists

cost redistribution
= the burden moves, hides, concentrates, defers, or becomes represented elsewhere
```

The question is not only:

```text
How much does this cost?
```

The question becomes:

```text
Which parts of the cost are visible, to whom, through what representation, and after what redistribution?
```

## Application Scope

Cost Visibility and Redistribution may apply wherever systems select, optimize, repeat, or govern configurations on the basis of partial cost visibility.

Relevant contexts include:

- AI-generated outputs
- search summaries
- synthetic media objects
- platform interfaces
- dashboard metrics
- institutional workflows
- automation systems
- agentic task traces
- public infrastructure
- organizational policies
- infrastructure governance
- platform governance
- public service configurations

In AI-mediated systems, low-friction generation can lower visible production cost while shifting validation, source checking, contextual alignment, legal responsibility, semantic fidelity review, and repair downstream.

In infrastructural systems, distributed benefits may coexist with concentrated resource burden, including energy, hardware, land, cooling, maintenance, grid demand, environmental load, and local system pressure.

In institutional workflows, visible process efficiency may depend on hidden coordination, correction, verification, and responsibility work carried by specific actors or roles.

In platform governance, dashboards, labels, scores, feeds, and metrics may make some costs governable while leaving other costs unrepresented.

## Non-Applicability

The Cost Visibility and Redistribution Model should not be used for every discussion of cost.

It does not apply well when:

- all relevant costs are already visible and comparable
- the analysis concerns only monetary price
- no redistribution across agents, layers, locations, systems, or time occurs
- the system has no decision or optimization structure
- the issue is simple accounting error
- hidden cost does not affect behavior, access, or default selection
- the analysis requires a normative theory of fairness rather than structural visibility
- the system explicitly removes a cost rather than shifting, hiding, deferring, or externalizing it

The model should not be used to claim that all redistribution is harmful.

Its domain is narrower:

```text
cost becoming actionable or non-actionable through visibility, representation, redistribution, and comparability
```

## Naming Declaration

The **Cost Visibility and Redistribution Model** originates within the Meta-Writing Ecology as a structural model for describing how cost becomes visible, hidden, redistributed, externalized, or locked into default behavior within multi-node systems.

The term **cost** in this model does not refer only to monetary expenditure. It refers to any burden generated by a configuration, including spatial, temporal, cognitive, coordination, verification, infrastructural, environmental, responsibility, and downstream repair burdens.

The term **visibility** does not mean simple transparency or disclosure. It refers to the condition in which a cost becomes structurally available as a decision-relevant, comparable, and actionable variable.

The term **redistribution** does not mean fair reallocation. It refers to the movement, partitioning, displacement, or externalization of cost across agents, layers, systems, locations, and time.

This model is not derived from economics, urban planning, behavioral finance, environmental accounting, labor economics, infrastructure policy, organizational design, or cost-benefit analysis, although intersections may exist.

The model does not prescribe optimal cost distribution.

It defines a narrower structural condition:

```text
systems optimize around costs that become visible,
while hidden costs shape behavior indirectly through burden, lock-in, breakdown, or delayed repair
```

This document constitutes the first OSF external-facing version of the Cost Visibility and Redistribution Model as a structural framework for analyzing perceived cost, total cost, cost opacity, redistribution, optimization constraints, and default state lock-in in AI-mediated, infrastructural, institutional, and meaning-bearing systems.
