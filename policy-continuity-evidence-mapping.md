## Minimal Formulation

Policy Continuity Evidence Mapping describes the condition under which evidence from multi-year policy systems is organized for planning transition through traceability, maturity classification, content-loss control, and human judgment.

It begins from three central distinctions:

```text
continuity
≠
repetition
```

```text
completion
≠
maturity
```

```text
AI-assisted mapping
≠
policy judgment
```

In compressed form:

```text
planning baseline
→ source stratification
→ evidence pool construction
→ policy-function mapping
→ maturity classification
→ traceability review
→ content-loss check
→ continuity judgment
→ planning transition
→ method disclosure
```

Most compressed:

```text
Policy continuity requires evidence that can be traced,
maturity that can be bounded,
and transition that remains human-judged.
```

The framework does not determine whether a policy succeeded.

It identifies how available evidence is positioned relative to planning intent, outcome maturity, source boundary, and future planning use.

## Core Characteristics

Policy Continuity Evidence Mapping treats continuity as a traceable relation between planning intent, reported outcome, maturity status, and future planning use.

Its core characteristics include:

- **Planning baseline** — the planning document, framework, or version that anchors review.
- **Source stratification** — separation of background context, direct planning evidence, completed execution reports, pending reports, and future planning materials.
- **Evidence pool construction** — inventory of relevant documents before evaluation or summarization occurs.
- **Policy-function mapping** — mapping of execution content to planning function rather than relying only on titles or topic labels.
- **Maturity classification** — differentiation among data establishment, method formation, tooling, institutionalization conditions, diffusion, and pending confirmation.
- **Evidence traceability** — recoverable linkage between a judgment and its documentary support.
- **Content-loss control** — recognition of what may disappear when large source sets are compressed into summaries or planning narratives.
- **Continuity judgment** — determination of what may be inherited, consolidated, redesigned, monitored, deferred, or excluded.
- **Planning transition** — movement from prior intent and reported outcomes toward future planning under uncertainty.
- **Human judgment boundary** — AI may assist organization, alignment, and draft mapping, but it does not own interpretation, maturity judgment, sufficiency determination, or planning priority.

The framework is not an audit procedure, performance evaluation, automated review system, or policy recommendation.

It is a public methodological orientation for keeping source position, maturity status, and responsibility boundary visible during planning transition.

## Structural Logic

Policy Continuity Evidence Mapping follows a recurring structural sequence:

```text
long-term policy system accumulates documents
→ planning versions, execution reports, and future planning materials overlap
→ ordinary summary loses traceability
→ completed activity is at risk of being mistaken for mature outcome
→ repeated terminology is at risk of being mistaken for continuity
→ AI-assisted alignment may create smooth but premature coherence
→ traceability, maturity, and content-loss controls are introduced
→ human reviewers bound the continuity judgment
```

The framework is activated when policy evidence must travel across planning cycles.

The structural risk is not only missing information.

The risk is premature closure:

```text
summary appears complete
→ traceability weakens

activity appears completed
→ maturity is over-claimed

term appears repeated
→ continuity is assumed

AI-generated alignment appears coherent
→ human judgment is displaced
```

The intended review order is:

```text
define baseline
→ identify source roles
→ map policy functions
→ classify maturity
→ retain traceability
→ check content loss
→ judge continuity
→ support planning transition
```

This order prevents source evidence from being flattened into general policy narrative before its role, maturity, and evidentiary sufficiency have been reviewed.

## Primary Patterns

### 1. Summary Substitution

A smooth summary substitutes for source-linked evidence.

```text
large source pool
→ compressed narrative
→ traceability loss
```

The review appears coherent while the source relation becomes weak.

---

### 2. Title-Proxy Mapping

A project title or report label is used as a proxy for substantive execution content.

```text
title suggests function
→ report mapped by title
→ actual content remains underread
```

The framework requires mapping by what the report substantively does.

---

### 3. Background Promotion

Background context is promoted into direct outcome evidence.

```text
pre-planning context
→ treated as implementation result
→ false continuity appears
```

Temporal stratification prevents contextual material from becoming achievement evidence.

---

### 4. Completion-Maturity Collapse

A completed report is treated as if it proves maturity.

```text
project completed
→ outcome treated as stable
→ maturity over-claimed
```

Completion means the report or project ended.

Maturity asks how far the result moved toward usable, repeatable, institutional, or diffused governance function.

---

### 5. Evidence Location / Sufficiency Confusion

A document contains relevant material, but that material may not be sufficient for the judgment being made.

```text
evidence located
≠
evidence sufficient
```

The framework separates finding possible evidence from determining whether it can support continuity, consolidation, redesign, or future planning use.

---

### 6. AI Judgment Transfer

AI-generated organization or mapping is treated as if it had determined policy meaning.

```text
AI aligns terms
→ mapping appears coherent
→ judgment boundary weakens
```

The framework preserves the distinction:

```text
AI may assist mapping.
Humans own interpretation.
```

---

### 7. Content-Loss Compression

Large evidence pools are compressed without tracking what was removed.

```text
reports summarized
→ details disappear
→ classification or transition judgment may change if lost content returns
```

Consequential loss includes omitted quantitative results, legal details, local variations, limitations, adoption evidence, technical appendices, or negative findings.

---

### 8. Future-Period Stabilization

Future planning material or incomplete later-period evidence is used to stabilize findings that remain pending.

```text
future plan mentions term
→ prior continuity assumed
```

Planning transition must not become mechanical extension.

---

### 9. Evidence Table Exposure

Case-level evidence tables are released without adequate source permission, privacy review, institutional boundary control, or case-specific disclosure decision.

The public framework can describe method orientation without exposing restricted evidence records.

## Distinction

Policy Continuity Evidence Mapping is not performance audit.

```text
performance audit
= evaluation of economy, efficiency, effectiveness, or compliance

policy continuity evidence mapping
= traceability, maturity, and transition positioning for planning-support purposes
```

It is not program evaluation.

```text
program evaluation
= assessment of outcomes, causality, effectiveness, implementation quality, or impact

policy continuity evidence mapping
= organization of evidence relative to planning intent and future planning use
```

It is not a statistical protocol.

```text
statistical protocol
= variables, measures, sampling, estimation, and inference design

policy continuity evidence mapping
= qualitative and structural traceability framework
```

It is not an automation framework.

```text
automation framework
= AI system executes review or judgment

policy continuity evidence mapping
= AI may support organization and alignment, while humans retain judgment
```

It is not an evidence table.

```text
evidence table
= output artifact

policy continuity evidence mapping
= framework governing how evidence tables may be constructed, interpreted, reviewed, and bounded
```

It is not a policy recommendation.

```text
policy recommendation
= statement of what should be done

policy continuity evidence mapping
= identification of what is traceable, mature, pending, insufficient, or transition-ready
```

It is not preregistration.

```text
preregistration
= recorded study plan before analysis

policy continuity evidence mapping
= public methodological note and protocol orientation for accumulated document review
```

It includes semantic alignment but extends beyond it.

```text
semantic alignment
= relation among terms, categories, plans, and reports

policy continuity evidence mapping
= semantic alignment + traceability + maturity + content-loss control + planning transition
```

## Application Scope

Policy Continuity Evidence Mapping may apply where policy evidence accumulates across multiple years, documents, institutions, and planning cycles.

Relevant contexts include:

- multi-year policy planning review
- strategic program review
- revised planning documents
- annual execution reports
- qualitative or semi-qualitative governance domains
- public-sector evidence synthesis
- institutional memory reconstruction
- planning transition support
- AI-assisted document organization
- policy systems with distributed execution evidence
- review settings where summary compression may produce content loss
- future planning processes that rely on incomplete but accumulating evidence

The framework is especially relevant when policy continuity depends on documentary accumulation rather than a single evaluation dataset.

It can also orient cases where later planning must begin before all later-period evidence has fully stabilized.

## Non-Applicability

Policy Continuity Evidence Mapping should not be applied to every policy review, document summary, or AI-assisted analysis.

It does not apply well when:

- no multi-year planning or transition condition exists
- the source set is small enough for ordinary direct comparison
- continuity is not a relevant policy question
- completion, maturity, and transition status do not need to be distinguished
- source traceability is already explicit and sufficient
- the review is a formal performance audit
- the review requires causal impact evaluation
- the task is statistical inference rather than structural evidence organization
- the task is simple administrative completeness checking
- AI assistance is not involved and no traceability risk is present
- policy recommendation is required without a separate evaluative framework
- restricted source materials cannot be handled within appropriate disclosure boundaries

The framework should not be used to claim that repeated policy language is invalid.

It should not be used to claim that completed projects lack value.

It should not be used to automate maturity judgment.

Its domain is narrower:

```text
multi-year policy systems in which planning evidence must be traced,
maturity must be bounded,
content loss must be controlled,
and transition must remain human-judged
```

## Naming Declaration

The term **Policy Continuity Evidence Mapping** originates within Meta-Writing Ecology as a methodological orientation for describing how evidence moves across multi-year planning systems without losing source position, maturity status, or responsibility boundary.

The term **policy continuity** does not refer merely to repeating earlier policy language, continuing existing projects, preserving institutional routines, or extending prior plans.

It refers here to the structured relation between prior planning intent, reported outcomes, evidentiary maturity, and future planning use.

The term **evidence mapping** does not refer only to listing documents, extracting quotations, creating an evidence table, or summarizing reports.

It refers here to the positioning of documentary evidence within a traceable planning function, maturity category, and transition judgment.

The term **maturity** does not refer to policy success, final effectiveness, causal impact, or institutional approval.

It refers to the observable readiness status of reported outcomes relative to later governance use.

The term **planning transition** does not refer to automatic continuation.

It refers to the structured movement from prior intent and reported outcomes toward revised planning decisions under uncertainty.

The term **AI-assisted** does not refer to automated evaluation, AI policy judgment, AI-generated recommendations, or machine decision-making.

It refers to document-organization support, semantic alignment support, evidence-location support, and draft mapping subject to human review.

This framework does not refer to any specific government, agency, institution, policy program, client, report set, or administrative decision.

It describes an abstract methodological structure for reviewing policy continuity in multi-year documentary systems.

This framework is adjacent to policy evaluation, program review, public administration, evidence synthesis, AI-assisted document analysis, strategic planning, knowledge management, and institutional memory work. It is not derived from those fields, although intersections may exist.

This document constitutes the first GitHub-facing public mirror version of Policy Continuity Evidence Mapping as a methodological note and protocol orientation for analyzing traceability, maturity, content-loss control, and planning transition in multi-year policy systems.
