## Minimal Formulation

False Legibility describes the condition in which a form becomes readable before it becomes structurally recognized.

It does not mean that nothing is visible.

It means that what is visible becomes prematurely stabilized as enough.

In compressed form:

```text
readable
≠
understood
```

Operationally:

```text
surface readability
→ premature recognition
→ inquiry suppression
→ consequential contact
→ reactive correction
```

The system fails not because the form was hidden.

It fails because the form became legible too early.

---

## Core Characteristics

False Legibility treats readability as a structural risk when surface recognition arrives before adequate understanding.

Its core characteristics include:

- **Surface readability** — a form presents enough familiar signals to be entered, handled, named, classified, or accepted.
- **Premature recognition** — the system treats a readable surface as sufficient evidence that the structure has been understood.
- **Structural misrecognition** — the operative nature of the form remains unresolved even though the surface appears clear.
- **Friction reduction** — readability lowers the felt need for further examination.
- **Inquiry suppression** — questions of origin, process, boundary, consequence, constraint, scale, referent, or operational role are prematurely reduced.
- **Operational handleability** — the form can be filed, routed, cited, summarized, displayed, approved, optimized, or used before it has been structurally recognized.
- **Trace deferral** — questions of source, transformation pathway, provenance, or production process are postponed because the surface is already usable.
- **Repair asymmetry** — correction becomes harder than initial acceptance once the readable form has circulated, been trusted, or entered institutional workflow.
- **AI-mediated intensification** — generative fluency, synthetic realism, dashboards, search summaries, platform labels, institutional templates, and agentic traces can produce readable surfaces at scale.

False Legibility is not a general suspicion of clarity.

It describes a narrower condition in which the wrong level of clarity becomes sufficient.

## Structural Logic

False Legibility follows a recurring structural sequence:

```text
form appears readable
→ interpretive friction decreases
→ surface is treated as adequate recognition
→ deeper inquiry is suppressed
→ contact or use proceeds
→ consequences accumulate
→ correction becomes reactive
```

The readable form may include:

```text
fluent language
recognizable formatting
expected category labels
institutional tone
visual realism
citation shape
interface smoothness
procedural sequence
statistical appearance
genre conformity
```

These signals create entry.

They do not guarantee structural adequacy.

The central asymmetry is:

```text
readable at the surface
≠
recognized at the structural layer
```

False legibility occurs when the first condition is mistaken for the second.

---

## Distinction

False Legibility is not unreadability.

```text
unreadability
= the system cannot easily read, identify, or handle a form

false legibility
= the system can read, identify, or handle the form too easily
```

False Legibility is not opacity.

```text
opacity
= relevant conditions are hidden, inaccessible, or difficult to inspect

false legibility
= the surface is visible enough to be mistaken for sufficient recognition
```

False Legibility is not factual error alone.

```text
factual error
= a specific claim is incorrect

false legibility
= readability substitutes for structural recognition
```

False Legibility is not misinformation alone.

It can occur without intentional deception, without explicit falsehood, and without a discrete false claim.

It can occur because the form is too successfully readable.

The question is not only:

```text
Can this be read, named, or handled?
```

The question becomes:

```text
Has this been structurally recognized, or has readability arrived too early?
```

## Relevant Interpretation Contexts

False Legibility may describe structural conditions where a readable surface enters use before its structure is sufficiently recognized.

Relevant contexts include:

- AI-generated answers
- search summaries
- synthetic images, video, or audio
- interface labels and platform badges
- dashboards and metrics
- institutional templates
- legal citations
- policy categories
- automated classifications
- agentic system traces
- machine-mediated information environments

Context relevance does not imply a deployed tool, operational method, audit framework, compliance system, professional service, domain-specific recommendation, or application claim.

In AI-mediated environments, false legibility is intensified because readable forms can be generated, formatted, summarized, labeled, and circulated at scale.

A generated answer may look explanatory before its sources are traceable.

A synthetic image may look evidentiary before its capture conditions exist.

A dashboard may look objective before its proxy structure is understood.

A search summary may look neutral before its compression has been examined.

An agentic trace may look procedurally complete before its action pathway is reconstructible.

## Relationship to Adjacent Models

False Legibility is adjacent to, but distinct from, several related Meta-Writing Ecology models.

```text
False Legibility
= premature readability at the level of entry and recognition

Premature Coherence
= premature closure at the level of system alignment and semantic resolution

Reality Consistency
= progressive testing of whether a structure continues to cohere across source, form, scale, exchange, process, and subject-position

Semantic Curvature
= deformation of interpretive space through accumulated coherence

Semantic Virology
= field-level transformation and propagation after structural contact
```

False Legibility can become one pathway into Premature Coherence.

A readable surface is accepted.

Inquiry decreases.

Variation is not explored.

Interpretation stabilizes early.

Coherence forms around an insufficiently recognized structure.

---

## Non-Applicability

The False Legibility Model should not be used for every case of readability.

It does not apply well when:

- the form is readable and structurally recognized
- the readable surface does not suppress inquiry
- the object is openly fictional or symbolic
- the issue is simple factual correction
- the problem is ordinary opacity rather than premature readability
- the system maintains adequate verification despite readable form
- the analysis depends only on subjective dislike of a surface

The model should not be used to claim that readability is inherently suspicious.

Its domain is narrower:

```text
readability that becomes accepted before structural recognition has occurred
```

## Naming Declaration

The **False Legibility Model** originates within the Meta-Writing Ecology as a structural model for describing what happens when something appears immediately readable while its operative nature remains structurally unread.

The term **legibility** in this model does not refer to actual comprehension. It refers to the successful appearance of recognizability, handleability, or interpretive availability.

The term **false** does not mean that every readable form is deceptive, incorrect, or intentionally misleading. It refers to a structural mismatch between surface readability and adequate recognition.

False Legibility occurs when a form becomes easy to identify, easy to handle, easy to categorize, or easy to accept before the system has adequately understood what it is dealing with.

This model is not derived from semiotics, perception theory, object analysis, communication failure studies, user-experience design, misinformation studies, media literacy frameworks, or ordinary readability theory, although intersections may exist.

The model does not ask why observers failed.

It examines how readability itself can become the first layer of misrecognition.

This document constitutes the first OSF external-facing version of the False Legibility Model as a structural framework for analyzing premature readability, surface clarity, inquiry suppression, and misrecognition in AI-mediated and meaning-bearing systems.
