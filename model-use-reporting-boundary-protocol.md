## Minimal Formulation

Model-Use Reporting Boundary Protocol defines when model-mediated participation becomes reportable without requiring full operational exposure.

The protocol begins from the distinction:

```text
model use
≠
reportable method
```

A model may participate in research, analysis, classification, interpretation, drafting, evaluation, transformation, deployment, or public-facing output.

That participation becomes reportable when the output is treated as inspectable, auditable, methodologically relevant, or structurally dependent on model-mediated conditions.

The protocol does not state that model use invalidates an output.

It states a narrower boundary:

```text
model-mediated output should not be treated as auditable
unless the conditions of model use are reportable
```

---

## Core Characteristics

- **Model participation** — the involvement of a model, tool, agent, retrieval system, automated system, or generative interface in a workflow.
- **Reporting boundary** — the point at which model participation becomes relevant to interpretation, auditability, source custody, or method description.
- **Auditability** — the ability to inspect the conditions under which an output was produced or transformed.
- **Source custody** — the preservation of source relation and input responsibility across model-mediated transformation.
- **Validation conditions** — the review, interpretation, correction, or human judgment that stabilizes an output after model participation.
- **Operational non-disclosure** — the distinction between reporting model participation and exposing full prompts, private materials, or internal workflows.

---

## Structural Logic

Model-Use Reporting Boundary Protocol follows a recurring structural sequence:

```text
model participates in a workflow
→ output becomes usable or public-facing
→ output is treated as inspectable or auditable
→ model conditions affect interpretation
→ reporting boundary is crossed
→ model participation must be made reportable
→ full operational exposure remains bounded
```

The central asymmetry is:

```text
reportable participation
≠
complete workflow disclosure
```

A model can be made methodologically visible without exposing every operational detail.

---

## Distinction

Model-Use Reporting Boundary Protocol is not an AI ethics doctrine.

It is not a model-use prohibition, validity guarantee, audit checklist, compliance framework, publication rule, or reproducibility standard.

It does not require the disclosure of all prompts, logs, private sources, tool settings, or internal registry decisions.

The protocol distinguishes:

```text
model use as incidental assistance
≠
model participation as reportable boundary condition
```

The relevant question is not whether a model was used in any way.

The question is whether model participation materially affects interpretation, evidence relation, source custody, validation, classification, or public output.

---

## Application Scope

The protocol may apply to:

- AI-assisted drafting
- model-mediated summarization
- automated classification
- retrieval-augmented workflows
- coding and transformation tasks
- analytic interpretation
- public documentation
- generated tables or reports
- interface-mediated outputs
- agentic workflows

It is most relevant when outputs become part of public records, research artifacts, institutional decisions, methodological accounts, or auditable documentation.

## Non-Applicability

This protocol should not be used when:

- model use has no material effect on interpretation, evidence, classification, or output
- the output is informal and not treated as inspectable
- reporting would falsely imply auditability
- full operational disclosure is demanded without boundary control
- the issue is general AI policy rather than method boundary
- the model output is already clearly marked as provisional

The protocol does not make all model contact reportable.

---

## Public Orientation and Method Boundary

This public protocol does not expose complete operational workflow.

It does not provide:

- complete prompt histories
- private source materials
- internal registry logic
- toolchain logs
- proprietary workflows
- compliance templates
- mandatory reporting forms

It defines a boundary for when model participation becomes reportable.

## Naming Declaration

The term **Model-Use Reporting Boundary Protocol** names a reporting protocol within Meta-Writing Ecology for identifying when model-mediated participation must be made reportable without requiring full operational exposure.

Within Meta-Writing Ecology, the term refers to the boundary condition formed by model participation, reporting obligation, source custody, interpretive accountability, and operational non-disclosure.

The term does not refer only to large language models, academic publishing, behavioural science, AI research, model evaluation, or experimental design. It may apply to any model-mediated workflow in which model participation affects meaning, classification, evidence, source custody, decision structure, or public interpretation.

It marks a shift from:

```text
model use as incidental assistance
```

to:

```text
model use as reportable boundary condition
```

The term preserves the distinction between making model participation reportable and making the full workflow, prompt history, private materials, internal registry, or operational infrastructure publicly available.
