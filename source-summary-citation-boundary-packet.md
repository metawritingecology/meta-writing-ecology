## Minimal Formulation

Source, Summary, and Citation Boundary Packet describes the condition in which source relations, summary surfaces, citation availability, retrieval access, and evidentiary grounding become conflated with source custody.

The packet begins from several distinctions:

```text
summary
≠
source

citation
≠
source access

retrieval
≠
grounding

availability
≠
validity

source relation
≠
source governance
```

The problem is not only hallucination, misinformation, fabrication, or source absence.

A source relation may be real, and the boundary failure may still occur.

The failure appears when surface-level proximity to a source becomes operationally sufficient while source custody remains unresolved.

---

## Core Characteristics

- **Source custody** — the accountable preservation of source relation, context, claim relation, and interpretive boundary.
- **Summary surface** — a readable condensation that may become operationally sufficient even when the source itself has not been encountered.
- **Citation boundary** — the distinction between pointing to a source and preserving its evidentiary role.
- **Retrieval access** — the availability of fragments, links, or indexed material without guaranteed grounding.
- **Summary displacement** — the condition in which an adequate summary replaces the source relation it should support.
- **Structural interpretability** — the conditions under which a source remains accountable, interpretable, and non-substitutable.

---

## Structural Logic

The packet follows a recurring structural sequence:

```text
source is summarized, cited, linked, retrieved, or indexed
→ readable reference surface forms
→ proximity to source appears sufficient
→ source custody is assumed
→ summary or citation substitutes for source relation
→ evidentiary grounding is overstated
→ source displacement stabilizes
```

The central asymmetry is:

```text
reference surface
≠
source custody
```

A citation, link, retrieved fragment, or summary may support orientation.

It does not automatically preserve the source relation.

---

## Distinction

This boundary packet is not a citation style guide.

It is not a bibliographic standard, archive protocol, metadata schema, fact-checking method, or source-verification checklist.

It distinguishes structural source custody from several visible substitutes:

```text
accurate summary
≠
source preservation

available citation
≠
source governance

retrieved fragment
≠
evidentiary grounding

catalogue presence
≠
interpretive custody
```

The issue is not whether a source exists.

The issue is whether the source relation remains structurally accountable.

---

## Application Scope

The packet may apply wherever sources are summarized, cited, retrieved, indexed, embedded, or made searchable.

Relevant contexts include:

- AI-generated summaries
- citation lists
- retrieval-augmented generation
- archive mirrors
- public record pages
- bibliographic surfaces
- search snippets
- source catalogues
- knowledge-base entries
- model-readable documentation

It is most relevant when a readable source surface begins to replace direct source relation.

## Non-Applicability

This packet should not be used when:

- ordinary citation practice is sufficient for the claim being made
- no source relation is being asserted
- a summary is openly provisional and non-substitutive
- retrieval is used only for orientation
- source custody is already preserved by context and method
- the issue is only incorrect citation formatting

The packet does not claim that summaries, citations, or retrieval are invalid.

It identifies the boundary at which they become substitutes for source custody.

---

## Public Orientation and Method Boundary

This public packet does not provide a complete source-verification procedure.

It does not include:

- audit checklists
- citation scoring methods
- archival governance rules
- complete verification workflows
- source-ranking systems
- metadata standards

It defines a boundary vocabulary for distinguishing source custody from reference surfaces.

## Naming Declaration

The term **Source, Summary, and Citation Boundary Packet** names a boundary packet within Meta-Writing Ecology for distinguishing source custody, summary formation, citation availability, retrieval access, and evidentiary grounding.

Within Meta-Writing Ecology, the term refers to the boundary condition formed by source relation, summary surface, citation availability, retrieval access, and source custody.

The term does not refer to a citation style guide, bibliographic standard, metadata checklist, archival protocol, or complete source-verification procedure.

It marks a shift from:

```text
citation or summary as sufficient source relation
```

to:

```text
source relation as a bounded custody condition
```

The term preserves the distinction between being able to summarize, cite, retrieve, or point to a source and having preserved the structural conditions under which that source remains interpretable, accountable, and non-displaced.
