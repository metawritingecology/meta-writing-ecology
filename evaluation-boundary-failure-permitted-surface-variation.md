## Minimal Formulation

Evaluation Boundary Failure under Permitted Surface Variation describes the structural condition in which an evaluated object remains within the permitted range while allowed surface variation becomes sufficient to move the evaluation outcome.

It begins from the central distinction:

```text
permitted surface variation
≠
evaluation-neutral variation
```

and:

```text
object stability
≠
evaluation stability
```

In compressed form:

```text
object remains within boundary
→ surface varies within permitted range
→ evaluator responds to the variation
→ outcome changes
→ boundary fails as evaluative control
```

Most compressed:

```text
The object remains inside the boundary.
The outcome moves anyway.
```

The framework does not claim that all surface variation is illegitimate.

It identifies the narrower condition in which allowed surface differences become outcome-moving despite no corresponding change in the evaluated object.

---

## Core Characteristics

Evaluation Boundary Failure under Permitted Surface Variation treats evaluation boundaries as outcome-control interfaces rather than only admissibility rules.

Its core characteristics include:

- **Object stability** — the evaluated object remains substantively stable within the relevant domain.
- **Permitted surface variation** — surface differences remain ordinary, defensible, acceptable, or formally allowed.
- **Evaluator sensitivity** — the evaluator responds materially to allowed surface differences.
- **Outcome movement** — the evaluation result, ranking, classification, priority, credibility, or institutional consequence changes.
- **Boundary-internal action** — no explicit crossing of the visible boundary is required.
- **Boundary-external effect** — the consequence exceeds what the boundary was designed to permit.
- **Surface compatibility reward** — the system begins rewarding fit with the evaluator's surface sensitivities.
- **Evaluation drift** — repeated surface sensitivity shifts the operative target of evaluation from object structure toward surface compatibility.
- **Robustness failure** — the boundary no longer preserves the relation between object stability and outcome stability.

The framework is not a theory of ordinary editing, stylistic improvement, presentation quality, or communication repair.

It is relevant when permitted surface variation produces outcome movement without corresponding object-level change.

## Structural Logic

Evaluation Boundary Failure under Permitted Surface Variation follows a recurring sequence:

```text
evaluation boundary established
→ permitted surface range remains available
→ object enters evaluation through a surface form
→ surface form varies within allowed range
→ evaluator responds materially to the variation
→ outcome moves without corresponding object change
→ participants learn which surfaces move outcomes
→ surface optimization becomes rational
→ boundary loses outcome-control capacity
```

The boundary may continue to appear intact.

No explicit rule is broken.

No prohibited action is required.

The surface remains allowed.

The evaluation process continues.

The result may appear procedurally valid.

The structural failure is that the boundary controls entry while failing to control sensitivity.

In compact form:

```text
admissibility controlled
+
sensitivity uncontrolled
=
boundary control illusion
```

Once allowed surface variation can move outcome, the system no longer evaluates only the object.

It also evaluates the object's compatibility with the evaluation surface.

## Primary Failure Patterns

### 1. Object-Stable / Evaluation-Variant Pattern

The object remains stable, but the evaluation varies.

```text
object stable
→ surface varies
→ evaluation changes
```

The core failure is evaluation variance under object stability.

---

### 2. Permitted Surface as Outcome Channel

A surface remains allowed but becomes a channel for moving the outcome.

```text
allowed surface
→ evaluator sensitivity
→ outcome channel
```

The system may continue to treat the surface as normal even after it becomes outcome-moving.

---

### 3. Surface Compatibility Reward

The evaluator begins rewarding compatibility with its preferred surface.

```text
surface compatibility
→ stronger evaluative response
→ object relation weakens
```

The object that best fits the evaluation surface may receive a stronger outcome even without corresponding object-level improvement.

---

### 4. Clarification-Leverage Ambiguity

A surface change may be described as clarification while also functioning as leverage.

```text
clarification claim
+
outcome gain
+
object stability
→ boundary ambiguity
```

The ambiguity appears because clarity can be part of valid evaluation while also becoming a path into disproportionate outcome movement.

---

### 5. Confidence Stabilization

A surface-sensitive outcome may not only move, but become more stable, confident, or internally coherent.

```text
surface variation
→ outcome movement
→ confidence stabilization
```

The shifted outcome may appear more reliable than the original.

---

### 6. Reason Reconstruction

After the outcome moves, reasons may reorganize around the new result.

```text
outcome movement
→ reason reconstruction
→ surface sensitivity becomes hidden
```

The system may generate explanations that make the moved outcome appear object-based.

---

### 7. Evaluation Targetization

Once participants learn which surfaces move outcomes, the evaluator itself becomes a target.

```text
surface sensitivity learned
→ evaluator optimized
→ evaluation targetized
```

The goal shifts from improving the evaluated object to improving the object's response profile inside the evaluation interface.

## Central Distinctions

### Permitted Surface Is Not Evaluation-Neutral

```text
permitted surface variation
≠
evaluation-neutral variation
```

A surface may be ordinary, defensible, professionally recognizable, procedurally compliant, or formally allowed while still functioning as an outcome-moving channel.

---

### Surface Acceptability Is Not Boundary Robustness

```text
surface acceptability
≠
boundary robustness
```

A boundary may allow variation because it assumes that variation is non-decisive.

If the evaluator treats that variation as decision-relevant, the boundary has misclassified the surface as safe.

---

### Boundary Violation Is Not Evaluation Boundary Failure

```text
boundary violation
= the object or action crosses an explicit rule

evaluation boundary failure
= the object remains within the rule while the outcome moves anyway
```

The outside is not produced by crossing the boundary.

It is produced by evaluator sensitivity inside the boundary.

---

### Surface Alignment Is Not Surface Leverage

```text
surface alignment
=
surface variation reduces distance between evaluation and object
```

```text
surface leverage
=
surface variation increases distance between evaluation and object while improving outcome
```

Not all surface work is structurally problematic.

The key question is:

```text
Did the evaluation move closer to the object,
or did the evaluator become more responsive to the surface?
```

---

### Outcome Movement Is Not Object Improvement

```text
outcome improvement
≠
object improvement
```

An evaluation outcome may improve because the object improved.

It may also improve because the surface became more compatible with the evaluator.

The framework describes the second condition.

---

## Boundary Robustness Condition

Boundary robustness is the capacity of an evaluation boundary to preserve outcome stability under value-stable surface variation.

The condition can be expressed as:

```text
if the object remains stable in the evaluation-relevant domain,
then permitted surface variation should not materially move the outcome
```

A boundary is weak when:

```text
surface variation is allowed
+
surface variation is treated as non-decisive
+
surface variation moves outcome
```

A boundary is stronger when:

```text
allowed variation is tested
surface sensitivity is known
outcome movement can be explained
object-level change is distinguishable from surface leverage
```

Robustness is therefore not only a property of rules.

It is a relation among object, surface, evaluator, and consequence.

## Relevant Interpretation Contexts

Evaluation Boundary Failure under Permitted Surface Variation may describe structural conditions where evaluation outcomes are mediated by surfaces.

Relevant contexts include:

- institutional assessment
- eligibility screening
- document-based evaluation
- platform ranking
- application review
- proposal evaluation
- quality assessment
- classification systems
- automated or semi-automated triage
- model-mediated review
- administrative processing
- compliance routing
- risk scoring
- credential evaluation
- public archive interpretation
- search-facing knowledge environments
- AI-mediated assessment systems
- organizational decision workflows
- machine-readable documentation environments

Context relevance does not imply a deployed tool, operational method, audit framework, compliance system, professional service, domain-specific recommendation, or application claim.

The framework is especially relevant where:

```text
evaluation has consequence
+
surface variation is permitted
+
object stability can be distinguished from surface variation
+
evaluator sensitivity is not fully controlled
+
participants can learn which surfaces move outcomes
```

It is less relevant where the surface itself is the primary object of evaluation, where presentation is explicitly the evaluated target, or where outcome movement is transparently tied to stated surface criteria.

## Non-Applicability

Evaluation Boundary Failure under Permitted Surface Variation should not be applied to every case of surface variation, editing, formatting, reframing, or presentation improvement.

It does not apply well when:

- the surface itself is the object being evaluated
- the evaluation criteria explicitly include presentation surface
- the surface change genuinely improves access to the evaluated object
- outcome movement is justified by object-level change
- the evaluator is expected to reward surface quality
- the issue is ordinary bias without a permitted-variation boundary
- the action clearly violates an explicit rule
- the surface variation has no evaluative consequence
- the analysis requires domain-specific legal, technical, or institutional adjudication
- no distinction can be made between object stability and surface variation

The framework should not be used to claim that editing is inherently suspect.

It should not be used to claim that presentation quality never matters.

It should not be used to automate accusations of gaming or manipulation.

Its domain is narrower:

```text
allowed surface variation that moves outcome
without corresponding object-level change
```

## Naming Declaration

The framework **Evaluation Boundary Failure under Permitted Surface Variation** originates within Meta-Writing Ecology as a structural model for describing how evaluation boundaries may fail while remaining formally intact.

The term **evaluation boundary** does not refer only to formal rules, thresholds, rubrics, eligibility requirements, procedural gates, or compliance criteria.

It refers here to the structural relation that should preserve alignment between evaluated object, evaluation process, and outcome consequence.

The term **permitted surface variation** does not refer only to writing style, format, polish, layout, metadata, interface design, or presentation.

It refers to any allowed variation in the surface through which an object becomes accessible to evaluation.

The term **boundary failure** does not imply explicit violation, fraud, bad faith, illegality, system crash, or visible procedural collapse.

It refers to the condition in which the boundary remains formally intact while losing the capacity to prevent outcome movement under value-stable surface variation.

The term **outcome movement** refers to a decision-relevant shift in classification, rank, priority, confidence, credibility, acceptance, scrutiny, routing, burden of proof, institutional status, or procedural consequence.

This framework is not derived from any single evaluation domain, platform study, institutional review setting, adversarial testing context, or assessment literature, although intersections may exist.

Within Meta-Writing Ecology, Evaluation Boundary Failure under Permitted Surface Variation functions as an evaluation-layer framework connected to Boundary Engineering, False Legibility, Surface–Structure Mismatch, Proxy Substitution, Cost Visibility and Redistribution, Responsibility Alignment, AI-Readable Knowledge Architecture, Provenance–Validity Separation, Reality Consistency, and Premature Circulation Diagnostics.

It names the condition in which the object remains inside the boundary, the surface remains permitted, and the outcome moves anyway.

This GitHub-facing version does not constitute the complete internal method, operational audit protocol, detection procedure, scoring rubric, compliance assessment, platform governance method, or client-facing diagnostic workflow.

It is a public conceptual mirror and citable orientation for Version 0.1 of the framework.
